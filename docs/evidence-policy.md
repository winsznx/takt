# Takt evidence policy

Takt's legal rules (IDs starting with `CA-`) come from the California Labor
Commissioner's published guidance, pinned in `legal/ca-dlse/rules/`. The rules
below (IDs starting with `TAKT-`) are Takt's own product policy. They decide
what Takt is willing to count. They are not law, and a hearing officer may
weigh evidence differently.

| Rule | Policy |
|---|---|
| `TAKT-DIFF-START` v1 | A start earlier than the employer's time record counts only when the worker confirms it **and** a schedule or a message independently supports it. Takt counts from the later of the worker's time and the earliest supporting record. |
| `TAKT-DIFF-END` v1 | Same as above for a later end. Takt counts until the earlier of the worker's time and the latest supporting record. |
| `TAKT-DIFF-MISSING` v1 | A worked day with no employer time record counts only when the worker confirms it and independent records support both its start and its end. |
| `TAKT-DIFF-PAID-HOURS` v1 | Hours on the employer's own time record are compared with hours paid on the wage statement for the same pay period. |
| `TAKT-DIFF-PAYSTUB-MATH` v1 | Each wage statement line is checked as hours × rate (× 1.5 for overtime). |
| `TAKT-ROUND-CENTS` v1 | Exact amounts are rounded half away from zero to the cent once per pay period and pay type. |

## Why a worker's own statement is not enough on its own

A worker's testimony is evidence in a real wage claim. Takt still refuses to
count time that only the worker's memory supports, because Takt's output is a
calculation other people will rely on, and it cannot judge credibility. The
worker's full statement is kept in the packet, and the uncounted part is
listed in plain words in the evidence index.

## What never counts

- A schedule by itself. A scheduled shift is a plan, not proof of work.
- A fact the worker has not reviewed.
- Facts from a file that duplicates one already added.
- A day where two employer records, or two schedules, disagree with each other
  and the worker has not resolved it.
